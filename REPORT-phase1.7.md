### M6: measured, then paced

**The limiter (read-only, from the Mac in Node, [limiter-probe.txt](docs/phase17/limiter-probe.txt)).** About 10 `getTransaction` calls per 10 s window on the public devnet node:

| probe | result |
|---|---|
| burst after 30 s quiet, one call at a time | 10 answered, the 11th HTTP 429 (3 of 3) |
| paced 60 s, after 30 s quiet | 0.5/s: 31 of 31 answered; 0.8/s: 49 of 49; 1.0/s: 60 of 61; **1.25/s: 10 answered, then 66 of 66 rejected** |
| does a rejected call prolong the block? | a burst, then probes once a second: first answer **18.4 s** after the block began; the same with 30 calls hammered in the first 10 s: **19.5 s** (twice each). Hammering prolongs the block by about a second, not materially; the block is about 18 s after the first rejection |
| batches after 30 s quiet | 5 elements: 5 answered (3 of 3); 10: 9 answered, 1 limited (3 of 3); 20: 9 answered, 11 limited (3 of 3). A batch element costs like a call |

**The reader.** One token bucket per `makeRpc` client, shared by single calls and batch elements; the public endpoints (`api.devnet.solana.com`, `api.mainnet-beta.solana.com`, `api.testnet.solana.com`) default to **0.6/s with a burst of 2**, any other endpoint starts at 200/s with a burst of 50; `{ rate, burst }` is configurable. A batch is never larger than the burst. A rate-limited answer empties the bucket and halves the rate, once per block (eight token intervals, between 1 and 20 s, so the probes that a block rejects do not halve it again); every answered element raises the rate by 10% up to the configured one. A rejected element waits for a token at the lower rate; a round that answered nothing is retried with one element. The fail-closed rules are unchanged. The first default was 0.8/s with a burst of 2, which is the 10-a-window quota exactly; the measurement below rejected it, which is why the default became 0.6/s and why a block now halves the rate once.

**The comparison (cold read of the H5 account, 250 dust credits, three readers alternating, [reader-compare-v1.txt](docs/phase17/reader-compare-v1.txt) and [reader-compare-v2.txt](docs/phase17/reader-compare-v2.txt)).** HTTP counts are what the wire saw; a "rate-limited answer" is a whole-request 429 or a limited element.

| reader | wall time | POSTs | elements sent | rate-limited answers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Phase 1.5, one by one (`142276f`) | 632.1 s, 645.2 s, 595.9 s | 685, 690, 647 | 685, 690, 647 | 429, 434, 391 |
| Phase 1.6, batches (`c521b31`) | 1169.6 s; **92.4 s and then gave up** (`complete: false`, 0 debits) | 645; 46 | 2693; 243 | 939; 89 |
| paced, first default 0.8/s (v1) | 562.1 s, 979.4 s | 150, 157 | 276, 283 | 20, 25 |
| **paced, 0.6/s, one halving per block (shipped)** | **451.6 s, 458.2 s** | 134, 135 | 260, 261 | **4, 5** |

The shipped paced reader is the fastest of the three on the public node, about **25% faster than one by one and 2.6 times faster than the batches**, with about a ninetieth of the rate-limited answers of the one-by-one reader (4 and 5 against 391 to 434). It is not fast: the limiter allows about 0.6 to 0.8 a second, and 253 transactions take 7 to 8 minutes. The 1.6 batch reader was the slowest, and in one of two reads it gave up and returned an incomplete history (fail closed, as designed). The first paced default was no better than the one-by-one reader on average (its second read took 979 s, after the rate had collapsed to the floor); that is what the one-halving-per-block rule and the lower default fix.

**On a keyed endpoint** (a key from a local env file, loaded without printing; only the host `devnet.helius-rpc.com` is printed; [reader-compare-helius.txt](docs/phase17/reader-compare-helius.txt)), the same cold read: one by one **30.8 s and 30.5 s** (256 POSTs), batches **1.9 s and 1.7 s** (14 POSTs), paced **1.4 s and 1.4 s** (14 POSTs); no rate-limited answer for any of them. So J2's benefit is real where the node does not throttle batches: **16 to 22 times** faster and 18 times fewer HTTP requests. On the public node it was a loss, and the pacing is what turns it into a gain.

- **The public node's limiter model** is a model: 10 calls per 10 s window and an 18 s block, from probes of one IP on one day. The shipped reader still met 4 to 5 rate-limited answers per cold read; it recovers, it does not avoid them entirely.
- **The empty-page `before` case behind a load balancer.** The public devnet node answers an error for an unseen cursor; a provider that answers `[]` would end a scan as complete. Not tested, not changed.

- **The legacy `agent.lock` check** treats a lock that names our own pid as stale (a container restart gets pid 1 again); a live process with the same pid in another pid namespace on a shared volume cannot be told apart.
- **Held reservations count against the current period whatever period they were reserved in** (M3 a). That is conservative on purpose: a payload kept in period 0 that landed in period 0 also blocks period 1 until it is proven expired or landed. A deployment with a long blockhash lifetime and short periods will notice.
